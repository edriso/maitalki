# Curriculum

The curriculum was distilled from the complete source folder in `/home/edriso/Documents/italki`: lesson and homework Markdown, text transcripts, accent-evaluation PDFs, business worksheets, pronunciation material, and supporting images/media. Binary audio/video was represented by its available transcript; private recordings and raw files are not shipped.

## Priority order

The accent evaluation supplied the primary sequence, refined by later lesson feedback:

1. **Core clarity:** stress/rhythm, American R, CH/SH, vowel A, and the later short I/E contrast.
2. **Message shape:** thought groups, meaningful pauses, intonation, TH, vowel U, and concise answer structure.
3. **Polish:** flap T, nasal resonance, wider vocabulary, and speed.

This order matters. A natural flap T does less for comprehension than clear rhythm or a contrast that changes one word into another.

## Recurring speaking framework

The lesson notes repeatedly point to the same practical system:

- Think in no more than three bullets.
- Lead with the answer, then a reason and a concrete example.
- Stress content words and compress function words.
- Divide long ideas into thought groups.
- Replace fillers with a deliberate pause.
- Practice twice: clear/careful, then natural.

## Sales framework

The product-specific reminders—“GAP,” “based on what you told me,” “make it about them,” “I hear you,” and “respect your time”—became one customer-first flow:

1. Open with purpose.
2. Discover current state, desired state, and impact.
3. Confirm what you heard.
4. Connect one capability to their stated outcome.
5. Advance to one clear next step.

This is reinforced by Salesforce guidance on [clarifying and confirming questions](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/relationship-selling/collaborate-with-the-customer), [active listening and mirroring](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/customer-centric-conversations-strategies/communicate-successfully-with-executives), and discovery that uncovers customer needs before a pitch.

The rhythm guidance is consistent with the U.S. Department of State’s overview of [thought groups, focus words, and stress](https://americanenglish.state.gov/files/ae/resource_files/etf_58_2_pg02-13.pdf). The use of deliberate silence is reinforced by Toastmasters’ guidance on [pauses for clarity and emphasis](https://www.toastmasters.org/magazine/magazine-issues/2019/july/silence-is-golden).

## Product boundaries

- “American” labels a specific pronunciation target; it is not presented as superior English.
- Clear communication is the goal, not erasing identity or promising “native” speech.
- Source worksheets are summarized into original examples rather than redistributed.
- New notes should earn their place through recurring feedback or a high communication impact.
